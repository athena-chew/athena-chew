# Section A - Exposure Summary
The commercial HVAC company is expected to receive a 3,400,000 EUR receivable from a developer in Milan 273 days from now. Due to changing rates between US and Euro exchange and borrowing, the total receivable in USD may heavily change over that time frame. A raise in EURUSD trade would cause our expected receivable to be worth more whereas drop in EURUSD trade would cause us to lose value on our receivable. In order to account for this change, we have three options for hedges, methods for keeping the rate at an indicated price, whether it be a future determined rate, a rate based on future exchange, or a floor minimum rate. The first option that is presented to is a forward hedge, which is a predictive rate based on exchange between two currencies at a later date. The forward hedge is given by the bank, and is 1.1756 EURUSD for our case. The money market similarly takes a borrow-convert-invest strategy, and gets a figure that is similar to the forward hedge, under parity among world currencies. The put option is a floor option that guarantees an exchange rate if the conversion rate drops, but takes the higher rate if it goes up. No matter what the change is, there is a premium that is paid. This exposure will document the results and recommendation of what method should be used based on the results of the model created and the live data fed into it through various sources pulled from November 10, 2026. 

# Section B - Hedge outcomes
The unhedged outcome has the biggest potential, both to lose and to gain. The amount received is linearly dependent to the increase or decrease of the exchange rate at the end of the 273 days. At the base case where the EURUSD rate remains the same, our conversation rate is $3,795,760, seen in C21 of the sensitivity tab of the model. This is the figure that will be used to compare other rates and how they would compare. The unhedged outcome has the lowest possible outcome and the greatest loss, with a possibility of $3,605,972, seen in C16 of the sensitivity tab of the model. It passes the put model at -2%, the money market model at +2% and then forward hedge predictably at 6%, as seen on the graph in the sensitivity tab of the model. 

The forward proposed hedge is the clear roof of the other values and settles at a price of $3,997,040, which is a $201,280 profit over the baseline. The indicative forward rate lies above the spot rate today by a large margin. This suggests that the market trade between the two is expected to go up over the timeframe. For out model, it stays above all 3 other models the entirety of the time. The values for the unhedged and the forward at +5% are $3,985,548 and $3,997,040, respectively, which are relatively close in value. This suggests that the unhedged most likely passes the forward hedge sometime after 5%. After that, it remains unhedged becomes worth more. 

The money market hedge lies flat as well, but only at $3,842,229. This is still higher than the baseline, drawing in a $46,469 profit. It's quickly outpaced by the unhedged model at just over 1% according to the graph in the model. At just 2% increase in rate, the unhedged model draws $3,871,675 compared to $3,842,229 by the money market hedge, a $29,446 difference. Against the put option, it falls after 4% increase, much later than the unhedged model. It stays at the second best throughout all of the losses in rate, but it ends up the 4th place in all scenraios where there is an increase in rate.

The put option creates a floor for any sensitivity under the current strike price, but allows for a reduced profit if it is gained. It begins by producing a profit over the unhedged, with it's floor of $3,716,966. Against the unhedged loss of $3,605,972 this is a save of $110,994 of lost proceeds. It is still only the third best option at this lowest sensitivity, with a difference of $125,263 and $280,074, respectively. It gets outpaced by the unhedged outcome at -2% and becomes the lowest yielding option between -2% and +3%. Only beyond 3% does it pass the money market hedge. It does not pass the forward hedge in the sensitivities that the model depicts. Due to it's characteristic nature, it does not pass the unhedged value for all positive values and thus will never be the best yielding option.
