# Section A - Exposure Summary
The commercial HVAC company is expected to receive a 3,400,000 EUR receivable from a developer in Milan 273 days from now. Due to changing rates between US and Euro exchange and borrowing, the total receivable in USD may heavily change over that time frame. A raise in EURUSD trade would cause our expected receivable to be worth more whereas drop in EURUSD trade would cause us to lose value on our receivable. In order to account for this change, we have three options for hedges, methods for keeping the rate at an indicated price, whether it be a future determined rate, a rate based on future exchange, or a floor minimum rate. The first option that is presented to is a forward hedge, which is a predictive rate based on exchange between two currencies at a later date. The forward hedge is given by the bank, and is 1.1756 EURUSD for our case. The money market similarly takes a borrow-convert-invest strategy, and gets a figure that is similar to the forward hedge, under parity among world currencies. The put option is a floor option that guarantees an exchange rate if the conversion rate drops, but takes the higher rate if it goes up. No matter what the change is, there is a premium that is paid. This exposure will document the results and recommendation of what method should be used based on the results of the model created and the live data fed into it through various sources pulled from November 10, 2026.

# Section C - Sensitivity interpretation
Under the EUR depreciation vs. appreciation as modeled through a -5% to 5% sensitivity in 1% steps within the model, only the money market and the forward hedge guarantee 100% certainty throughout any change in rate. They both stay at $3,842,229 and $3,997,040, respectively, for all possible sensitivity outcomes. This leads them to have a flat rate throughout the graph. The put option has a certainty of 100% only when there is a loss. Because the premium is paid out as a cost at any sensitivity, this certainty is only useful at -2%, where the unhedged loss becomes greater than the premium cost baseline. At all positive sensitivities, the put option is uncertain but it is a guaranteed growth. It grows linearly as the sensitivity increases, eventually passing the money market model at 4%. It grows at the same rate as the unhedged rate, so it will also never pass the unhedged rate. The unhedged model has the most uncertainty, and it it completely linearly dependent on the change in sensitivity. This causes major losses if the rate drops significantly but major gains if it increases significantly. 

In terms of flexibility, the forward hedge offers the clear most flexibility in terms of potential gain or loss. It guarantees the most money earned throughout any of the 11 scenarios, and is able to provide benefit no matter what the market does within those 11 scenarios. The money market hedge offers the next best flexibility. This is because it produces the second most money in 7 of the 11 scenarios, and is the third best in 2 additional scenarios. This includes the baseline, where it produces $46,469 more than the current striking price. This allows for the second most flexibility in terms of beneficial profit. The unhedged model is the next best flexibility, since it produces the second most money in 4 scenarios and the third most in 4 scenarios. It offers a slightly low probability of it producing the best profit, with it being within the bottom two most money in 6 of the scenarios, more than half of the time. Finally, the option hedge is the least flexible, producing only the third best money in 5 of the 11 scenarios. In the rest of the time, it produces the least amount of profit, over 50% of the time, almost guaranteeing a loss compared to the flat models. 

In terms of cost compared to the unhedged model, the money market never produces a cost as it never goes beneath the unhedged model. For the money market, there is no initial cost to it, and it only produces cost when the unhedged model produces more profit. This happens at +2%, where the unhedged model surpasses the profit gained from the money market model. The maximum cost difference from the unhedged is $143,319 at +5%, but comparative to the most profit gained, $236,257 at -5%, this is not very costly. The cost is the most with the put model, which consistently has a cost of $78,794 for all profit gained. This cost is only negated when a loss occurs, and only after -2% does this cost outweigh the loss that would be made with the unhedged model. At it's peak gain from this cost, it saves $110,994 of loss compared to the unhedged model. 

# Section D - Recommendation
Across all of the metrics that were observed, the unanimous best strategy across all sensitivities tested is the forward hedge. It remains the most profiting among all 11 scenarios, even outlasting the growth of the put and the unhedged models. At it's closest variance from the next best model, it is still $11,492 more profiting. This occurs at +5% sensitivity, between the forward hedge and the unhedged model, with the forward hedge being more profiteering. It can be visually deduced that the unhedged model only passes at about +6%, but this is not modeled. Even among the sensitivity metrics, it is 100% certain to produce a profit, it provides the most flexibility across any of the sensitivities, and there is no cost against at any of the sensitivities. 

The CIP-implied forward and the bank quoted forward are very far from agreeance. The banks forward quote is 1.1756 and the CIP is 1.1301. This is a rate gap of 0.0455, which translates to a monetary gap of $154,700. This is enough to shoot the forward hedge way above every other hedge and unhedged model across all 11 sensitivities. The reason that this gap is so high is unknown, since under parity this gap should be closer to 0.05%, whereas right now it sits at 3.87%. The only reason that it is far off that I could reason is that the forward was given at a time where rates were much better, and has since sunk significantly, leading to a forward rate that is much higher than the current reflected rate. This gap is exactly what allows us to make this decision so easily, since we are guaranteed to make money.

In the case that parity holds and the bank implied forward around the same as the money market, then there would be a closer competition between the models. In this world, the recommendation that would be the most advantageous would be between the flat models, the forward hedge or the money market hedge, whichever one is greater. This is because according to the money market hedge, it is the better profit in 6 of the 11 sensitivities, including the base case. They also both provide complete certainty and flexibility among all of the sensitivities since they are flat models. The only time that they outperform the unhedged is in sensitivities +2% to +5%, and the further only outperform the put option from +4 to +5%. In either case, the flat model still produces more and is more certain, so they would be the recommended choice.
